# Dictation

One person knows what they want and says it, by voice or by typing it out in one go.
The text goes into `raw/` as it was said.

## When it fits

- The idea is in one head and needs to get out quickly.
- The person who dictates is also the one who decides.
- Rough wording is fine; precision comes from grilling.

## Steps

1. **Dictate.** Speak or type the idea as it comes. Do not structure it into
   requirements.
2. **Save it into `raw/` as it was said,** including half sentences. A voice note
   goes in as it is; the agent derives a text copy to work from.
3. **Grill.** Before it writes any page, the agent asks about what the dictation left
   vague, one question at a time. Most of the precision comes from this step.
4. **Capture.** The agent writes the pages, then puts the questions the writing threw
   up to you in a numbered closing round, and in a further round if an answer opens a
   new question.

## What lands in `raw/`

The dictated text, unedited, with the date. For a voice note, the recording is the
original; the transcript is a derived copy.

## Who decides, and where it is recorded

The person who dictated. The dictation records the intent, the grilling records the
precise decisions.

## Pitfalls

- **The dictation is tidied before it is saved.** Then the original intent is lost,
  and later questions cannot go back to it.
- **Nobody else is asked.** A dictation is one person's view. If others are affected,
  follow it with an issue or a workshop.
